 # Research Log — Day 1
Date: October 11, 2026

## My research interests
I am interested in Artificial Intelligence, particularly reliable AI agents, Large Language Models (LLMs), Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), and AI systems.

Through my previous projects and research publications involving RAG chatbots and AI agents, I have become interested in understanding not only how to build AI applications, but also how to make them more reliable, accurate, and efficient.

I want to investigate how AI agents make mistakes, why those mistakes happen, and how we can detect or prevent them before they affect users. I am especially interested in tool-using AI agents that interact with external tools or systems to complete tasks.

## What I want to learn
1. How to evaluate AI agent reliability using measurable metrics and reproducible experiments.
2. How to identify common failures, such as incorrect tool selection, invalid arguments, and unsupported answers.
3. How to detect errors while minimizing false alarms, latency, and computational cost.
4. How to read papers critically, identify research gaps, and formulate testable hypotheses.
5. How to conduct independent AI research and communicate findings through reports and publications.

## Initial research question
Can a validation method detect incorrect tool calls made by AI agents while avoiding false positives on valid tool calls?

## Initial plan
Build a small benchmark using synthetic tool-call examples, implement a basic validation method, compare it with a stronger verifier, and analyze the results using appropriate metrics.

## Long-term goal
Develop strong AI research skills, contribute to reliable AI systems, and prepare for a fully funded PhD in Computer Science or Artificial Intelligence in the United States.
